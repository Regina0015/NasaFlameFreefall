# The two views

A switch changes the view. Findings, evidence and grades are the same; only how they are presented changes.

| | Student view | Professional view |
|---|---|---|
| For whom | Undergraduate students and the curious public | Graduate students, teachers, researchers, aerospace engineers |
| Language | Plain, with a glossary | Technical, with units and conditions |
| Sees first | What the finding means and why it matters | The finding with values, number of tests and tested range |
| Confidence grade | A color and one sentence ("checked against 129 tests") | The full list of checks with their results |
| Proof chain | Summarized: where it came from | Complete: calculation, parameters, rows and DOI |
| Data | Charts | Filterable table and CSV download for modeling |
| Investigations | What burned, where and why | Objectives, hypotheses, variables, sensors, hardware, publications |
| Extra | Context stories: Apollo 1, Apollo 13, Mir; uses on Earth | Side-by-side comparison of investigations; knowledge gaps |

## Rules

- Neither view shows a claim without its grade.
- The student view simplifies the language, not the truth: if the grade is low or there is no data, it says so too.
- The texts of both views come from the same audited findings (`summary_student` and `summary_professional`).
