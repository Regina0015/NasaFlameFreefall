# Flame in Freefall — challenge summary (Space Apps 2026)

Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/flame-in-freefall/?tab=details (read 2026-11-14).
Official title: "Flame in Freefall: SI-Powered Fire Safety Insights from Microgravity Combustion Data". Difficulty: intermediate / advanced. Theme: space exploration.

## What it asks

An **AI-powered web dashboard** that **summarizes, ranks and interprets** NASA's microgravity combustion investigations (the **PSI**, Physical Science Informatics, repository) to deliver **fire-safety** insights for human exploration.

Optionally, the user can:
1. Find the investigations most relevant to each crewed environment: transit vehicle, space station, lunar or Mars base, pressurized rover.
2. Extract data useful for **predictive flammability models** and fire scenarios.
3. Highlight research that helps design fire-safety controls and equipment.
4. (Extra) Applications on Earth: water-mist extinguishers, smoke detectors, firefighter equipment.

Out of scope: rockets and propellants.

## Considerations (optional)

- Audience: teachers, graduate and undergraduate students, researchers and aerospace engineers.
- Attributes to show: objectives, fuel (sample or burner form and size), conditions (% O2, air speed), variables (what changes and what repeats between tests), sensors or measurements.
- Compare similarities and differences between investigations.
- Hardware families:
  - ACME (6 experiments, gases, CIR): includes BRE, CFI-G (up to 85% O2) and E-FIELD Flames (imitates partial gravity).
  - MDCA (liquid fuel droplets, CIR): FLEX, FLEX-2, CFI.
  - MSG + SPICE flow duct (solids and gases): BASS, BASS-II, SPICE, SLICE.
  - Others: Saffire-I/II/III (solids), SAME and SAME-R (smoke detection), DAFT and DAFT-2 (dust).
- Prevention, detection and suppression: NASA's three fire-safety pillars.
- Lunar base atmosphere: 30% O2 or more at 10.2 psi or less (40% more O2 than on Earth, so materials are more flammable). Spacesuit: 100% O2 at 4.3 psi.
- PSI has no partial-gravity combustion data (LUCI and FM2 will come later). Microgravity and normal-gravity results can serve as bounds to estimate intermediate gravities.
